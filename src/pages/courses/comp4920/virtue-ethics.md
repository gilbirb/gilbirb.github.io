## **Introduction**
***

The Problem with Other Theories: Consequentialism (judging actions purely by outcomes) and Deontology (following strict moral rules) turn ethics into algorithmic formulas or rule checklists. By trying to calculate or automate right and wrong, they risk stripping out the human element of morality.

Virtue Ethics argues that morality cannot simply be outsourced to a step-by-step procedure, formula, or rigid rulebook.

While consequences and rational rules matter, they don't cover everything. Real moral life requires space for character traits and human emotions such as kindness, empathy, compassion, loyalty, and love.

Instead of asking "What formula tells me what to do?", Virtue Ethics asks "What kind of person should I become?"
## **Virtue Ethics**
***

Core Goal (Character over Rules): Morality is not just about rational arguments or checklists; its main purpose is cultivating a person's inner moral core and building good character. A good person is someone with good behavioural habits (dispositions).

Rationality of the Passions: Emotions ("passions") sit right at the centre of morality. Our feelings naturally move us to act before we even formulate logical reasons, helping us recognise what the right action is.

The Doctrine of the Mean: Every virtue sits in the sweet spot between two extremes:
* Too little of an emotional reaction leads to a vice of deficiency.
* Too much leads to a vice of excess.
* Virtue means having the right emotional response in the right amount for the specific situation (e.g., handling the emotion of fear properly produces bravery, balanced between cowardice and recklessness).

Context & Lifelong Practice: Character development never truly ends. Different roles call for different character traits (e.g., what is appropriate for a firefighter differs from a teacher).

Strengths: 
* Matches our everyday human moral intuition and prevents the cold, extreme outcomes that purely formulaic ethical theories can cause.

Weaknesses: 
* Can come across as elitist and fails to provide neat, straightforward answers when you need to know exactly what to do in a specific dilemma.

## **Conclusion and Looking Forward**
***

Be Cautious When Judging Morality: In Virtue Ethics, moral worth depends on both a person's actions and their inner motivations

The Problem of Perception: You cannot directly observe or measure someone else's true motivations with your normal physical senses. Discerning genuine character requires subtle emotional sensitivity and intuition, making moral judgements about others delicate and prone to error.

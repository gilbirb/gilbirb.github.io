## **Human-Centred Design**
***

Human-centred computing extends beyond basic usability to multi-stakeholder considerations and serving human flourishing. It addresses algorithmic and delegated decisions (e.g., automated content moderation, feed ranking) where computational systems shape human experiences at scale.

**Evolution of HCC**:

* 1970s Participatory Design: Workplace democracy where workers co-design systems.
* 1980s User-Centred Design (Norman): Fits the design to the user via personas and usability testing, but can overlook individuals outside predefined personas.
* 1990s Value-Sensitive Design (Friedman et al.): Treats values as core stakeholders in design alongside people.
* 2010s Design Justice (Costanza-Chock): Shifts focus from "users" to "publics," arguing affected communities should lead design.
* 2020s: More-than-human and decolonial frameworks.

## **Technological Determinism vs. Social Constructivism**
***

* Determinism: Technology drives societal change and history autonomously (e.g., "AI will inevitably replace all jobs").
* Constructivism: Human choices, policy, and social needs shape technology and its adoption (e.g., "The QWERTY keyboard layout", "AI impacts depend on worker protection laws").

## **Value-Sensitive Design (VSD) Framework**
***

**Tripartite Methodology**:
* Conceptual Investigations: Surfacing values and identifying what is at stake for direct and indirect stakeholders.
* Empirical Investigations: Researching how affected individuals understand and prioritize those values in practice.
* Technical Investigations: Analyzing how technical features instantiate or undermine those values.

**Key Values Identified**: Human welfare, ownership/property, privacy, freedom from bias, universal usability, trust, autonomy, informed consent, accountability, and environmental sustainability.

## **Human Research Ethics**
***

When Ethics Approval is Needed:
* Required by a Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC) whenever interacting with human participants (surveys, interviews, usability testing), collecting identifiable data, reusing datasets outside their original consent scope, observing online communities covertly, or conducting experiments impacting participant wellbeing or reputation.

### Historical Origins & Core Codes

* Nuremberg Code (1947): Established that voluntary, informed consent is essential; participants retain the right to withdraw; risks must not exceed anticipated benefits; and experiments require qualified researchers.

* Declaration of Helsinki (1964): Developed by the World Medical Association to govern medical and clinical research, emphasizing privacy, vulnerable groups, and protocol registration.

* Tuskegee Syphilis Study (1932–1972): Infamous study where treatment was withheld from 400 African-American men without informed consent to observe disease progression; serves as a primary historical case of medical racism and exploitation of vulnerable populations.

## **The Belmont Report**
***

Three Foundational Principles
1. Respect for Persons: Upholding autonomy and protecting individuals with diminished autonomy.
2. Beneficence: Maximizing benefits while minimizing potential harms.
3. Justice: Ensuring fair distribution of research burdens and benefits without exploiting vulnerable groups.

Core Applications: Translates principles into practice via Informed Consent (information, comprehension, voluntariness), Assessment of Risks and Benefits, and Fair Selection of Subjects.

## **Tech Platform Research Controversies**
***

* LinkedIn Social Experiments (2022): Tested the "strength of weak ties" on 20 million users over 5 years via algorithm changes in "People You May Know," raising concerns over potential impacts on user livelihoods.
* Facebook Emotional Contagion Study (2014): Altered news feeds of ~689,000 users to test psychological contagion without explicit informed consent (relying solely on Terms of Service), sparking widespread debate over IRB oversight in private industry research.

## **Research Integrity & Professional Practice**
***

### Australian Governance & Foundational Value

Governed by the Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research (2018), which establishes that high-quality, credible, and trustworthy research is essential for public trust and societal benefit.

Real-world benefits are illustrated by major Australian innovations (e.g., WiFi, Google Maps, Cochlear implants, PERC solar cells, ultrasound scanners).

### Research Misconduct & Consequences

Highlights cases tracked by Retraction Watch, demonstrating the long-term harms of fabrication, falsification, and poor methodology:
* Andrew Wakefield’s debunked 1998 autism-vaccine paper (fraud that continued to misinform for decades).
* Carlo Croce (cancer research lab investigated for plagiarism and data falsification).
* Scott Reuben (anesthesiologist who fabricated data across dozens of papers, served prison time, yet had retracted papers cited for years).

## **Key Practical Issues & Human-Centred Design Recommendations**
***

Core Compliance Areas: Participant recruitment, informed/voluntary consent, assessing risk, digital data collection/privacy, and confidentiality.

Actionable HCD Practices:
1. Map stakeholders explicitly.
2. Surface value tensions before writing code.
3. Design contestability paths for affected people.
4. Engage the Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC) early as a design partner.
5. Document data provenance and label training-set consent.
6. Publish a value-explicit design memo alongside model cards.
